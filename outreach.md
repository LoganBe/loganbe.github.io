# What is Neuroscience?

<div class="container-fluid p-0 m-0 mb-4">
Neuroscience is an interdisciplinary field that focuses on studying the structure and function of the brain. The brain harbors an immense number of mysteries that we still do not yet understand such as: <br><br>
1) What causes neurological issues such as epilepsy or Alzheimer’s? <br>

2) How does the brain process information about the environment? <br>

3) What is consciousness? <br> <br>

These questions have inspired people from a variety of fields of study—such as biology, physics, psychology, computer science, engineering, and mathematics—to come together in the attempt to understand how the brain works.
</div>
---
# Our Mission
<div class="container-fluid p-0 m-0 mb-4">
We want to bring Neuroscience to you! The brain is something we all have, and we should all become a little bit more familiar with it. That is where we come in. <br><br>

At the University of Texas at Austin, we have a committed and dedicated group of graduate students from the Institute of Neuroscience who aim to share their knowledge about the brain with everyone. These are students who have extensively studied the brain and are currently conducting their own research! <br><br>

We offer a free program called <strong> NeuReach </strong>, open to all schools and ages, with hands-on Neuroscience demonstrations and presentations to educate and inspire students to pursue STEM education.
</div>

## What We Offer

### 1. Neuroscience Demonstrations
<div class="container-fluid p-0 m-0 mb-4">
Ever want to take control of another person’s nervous system? Or explore brain anatomy up close? <br>

Through our neuroscience demonstrations, students get hands-on exposure to neuroscience—deepening their understanding of brain physiology and function. <br> <br>

<strong> Length </strong>: Three 15-minute demonstrations, rotated in groups of 3  <br>
<strong> Location </strong>: A large space for movement and easy access to stations  <br>
<strong> Target Audience </strong>: All ages  <br><br>

<strong>Activities:</strong> <br>
<ul>
<li>
<em> Brain Anatomy --  </em>
  Students explore sheep brain anatomy, guided by an expert. Students can examine real brains with gloves and ask questions. <br>
</li>
<li>
<em>Electrical Activity of Nerves -- </em> 
  Students observe EMGs of their own muscles and even participate in a demo where one person’s muscle activity controls another’s arm via nerve stimulation. <br>
</li>
<li>
<em> Visual Adaptation -- </em>  
  With prism goggles distorting vision, students throw balls into a bin and witness how quickly the brain adapts to new sensory inputs. <br>
</li>
</ul>

### 2. "The Scientific Method"
<div class="container-fluid p-0 m-0 mb-4">

The scientific method has been used since the 17th century and remains foundational to how we explore the world. It's not always a linear process and can be adapted depending on the context. <br><br>

In this talk, we:
<ul>
<li> Break down the scientific process </li>
<li>  Discuss how it's applied in modern neuroscience </li>
<li>  Show real-world examples from current UT Austin research </li>
</ul> <br>

<div class="text-center">
<img src="/assets/images/expdesflow.png" width="400" alt="Scientific Method Flowchart" class="img-fluid">
</div>

<strong> Length </strong>: 1 40-minute presentation  <br>
<strong> Location </strong>: Classroom with HDMI projector <br>  
<strong> Target Audience </strong>: Elementary or middle school students <br>

</div>

### 3. "Tips and Tricks for Becoming a Scientist"
<div class="container-fluid p-0 m-0 mb-4">

Becoming a scientist is a unique path for everyone. In this talk/discussion, graduate students from diverse academic backgrounds share their personal journeys into science. <br> <br>

Topics include: <br>
<ul>
<li> Choosing a scientific field </li>
<li>  Getting involved in research </li>
<li>  Navigating undergrad and grad school </li>
<li>  The ups and downs of academic life </li>
</ul> <br>
We aim for open conversation, students are encouraged to ask questions and explore how to pursue their own scientific aspirations.

<strong>Length</strong>: 1 60-minute discussion  <br>
<strong>Location</strong>: Panel-style room setup (optional projector)  <br>
<strong>Audience</strong>: High school students
</div>
